## 3. Feasibility

**Verdict: feasible as a staged project; high risk as a single big-bang build.** The mechanics are standard for the genre. The risk is content volume: 52 chapters, about 20 locations, about 20 boss and set-piece encounters, and 20 to 26 hours of play.

| Dimension | Assessment | Main risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | Low to medium risk. Third-person combat, companion AI, vehicles, puzzles and checkpoints are well-understood | Companion AI that gets stuck, chase sequences, the cover-up and disguise AI | Build and test these systems in the slice first |
| Content | High risk. Volume is the main threat | Art, animation and level production cannot keep up | Level kits, three chapter cost tiers, episodic release, reuse of hubs |
| Schedule and cost | Medium to high risk | Overrun from scope creep | Stage gates, fixed Definition of Done, cut-list ranked in advance |
| Legal and IP | Low if licence covers games, voices and music; high if not | Missing rights block release | Written confirmation before production; keep the bible's name variants in the glossary |
| Story accuracy | Medium risk. Season 2 is only partly verified | Wrong story beats | Episode check gate and the build check on [I] tags |
| Team | Depends on the team | Missing specialist roles | See section 13 |

**Scope options**

| Option | What ships | Planning effort (rough, person-years) | When it makes sense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: Full game | All 52 chapters in one release | 45 to 75 | A funded studio of 15 or more with a 3 to 4 year runway |
| B: Episodic (recommended) | Slice, then Part One in three drops (chapters 1 to 9, 10 to 18, 19 to 26), then Part Two in the same way | 8 to 15 for the slice plus Episode 1; 25 to 40 for all of Part One | Most teams; money and learning arrive early |
| C: Slice only | Chapters 1, 5 and 7 as a demo | 1.5 to 3 | Pitching, funding, validating the market |

The effort ranges are rough planning figures based on typical indie productivity for stylised 3D action-adventure with reuse, not quotes. A small team should expect to cut chapter length to 15 to 20 minutes and lean on reuse.

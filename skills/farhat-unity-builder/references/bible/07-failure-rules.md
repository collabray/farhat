## 7. Failure rules

The show is family-friendly, so the game never uses death screens. Failure is a *setback* that costs time, resources or an ability, then returns the player to a fair checkpoint with a short line from Ben, Wings or Tilda.

| Failure type | Trigger | Consequence | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health failure | Hearts reach zero | Farhat is knocked out, fade to the last checkpoint | Companion line, resume |
| Objective failure | A rescue, escort or timer runs out (Shadow dissolve meter in ch. 5, Ben's cousins in ch. 20, Nigel's mine in ch. 25) | Scene restarts | Retry, with a one-time optional hint after the second failure |
| Stealth failure | Detected in an infiltration (ch. 2, 11, 29) | Short chase, or reset to the stealth start | Retry, or defeat the pursuers |
| Chase failure | Hit too many times in a balloon or flying chase (ch. 7) | Vehicle damaged, restart from last gate | Retry |
| Puzzle failure | A decode, antidote or lab puzzle fails (ch. 14, 15, 43) | Reset puzzle, never combat penalty | Retry |
| Impulse trap (story-gated) | The player picks the impulsive option in a scripted choice, such as turning back for his mother in ch. 17 | A resource loss and a temporary ability lock (for example, the Shadow disabled for the next level), but the story still proceeds as in the show | Recovered by a later chapter beat |
| Corruption failure (Season 2) | Standing too long in Namirash zones | Controls partly reverse until the player leaves the zone | Leave the zone, or use a light item |

**Fail-forward rule:** no failure can cause permanent loss of the story. The canon outcome always occurs; the player's skill decides how clean the path is (rewards, ratings).

**Chapter ratings:** each chapter ends with a light-star rating (one to three) based on health lost, time taken and optional items found. Stars unlock concept art, music and cosmetics, not progression.
